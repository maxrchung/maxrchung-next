bs? is our take on BS Poker (sometimes known as Liar's Poker). It's a bluffing
card game where you deal out a few cards to a group, then you take turns trying
to guess the better poker hand you can complete with everyone's cards. If you
don't think a hand exists, you call BS on it.

There are a lot of house rules you can add. In our bs? we added a group voting
system so everyone individually decides BS or not. We also keep track of a score
depending on hand rarity and determine a winner after some rounds are done.

This final version is in a bit of a weird state. The itch.io release is an older
build with a few patches. The latest in main has some issues with parts of the
game loop. Long story short is rewriting stuff took a long time, and we couldn't
get things fixed by the deadline.

bs? was made over a 3 day weekend. We used Godot and GDScript for the client and
C# for the WebSockets server.

## Background

Maybe a tiny background recap since I started writing something in the intro and
it kinda ballooned. Overall, this game jam wasn't as smooth as previous ones.
Day 1, We struggled a bit to come up with an idea. BS Poker was our backup plan
after we couldn't figure out some kind of gameplay that could possibly work with
Mpreg. I worked on card assets Day 1 and got them into Godot. Not terribly
productive but at least refamiliarizing myself with everything.

Day 2 I decided to focus solely on client to get something connected and did a
lot of prototype-y stuff in Godot/GDSCript. By Day 2's end I had a pretty cruddy
UI that was talking and updating things with the server. Maybe a mistake, but I
worked throughout the night to try and make this look as good as I could by
polishing UI. That left me really dead and pretty much useless the last day save
for doing a small things to figure out how we would deploy this (Railway).

In my head, I thought things were in a good spot for handoff Day 3. I was pretty
happy with the state of it and thought things would just be additions to what I
laid some foundation for. But Julie didn't like the messy UI I wrote, that was
basically cramming everything into one scene. Admittedly it can be painful to
follow, but Julie's solution was to rewrite everything to separate out into
multiple scenes. I'm not opposed to that, but if you're going to rewrite you
better leave things in a stable state.

And lo and behold, end of Day 3, things weren't connected properly and arguably
in a worst state than my prototype version. Game loop had issues, some game
crashes, release version had some major visual bugs. Granted, I could have
helped out more on Day 3 to try and rewrite things, but with the sleep crash
affecting me and not feeling motivated to migrate perfectly working code into
something brand new, I didn't do much.

In some defiance, the version I'm publishing on itch.io is my prototype with a
few patches. Personally I think it's a more complete game in the bug-free sense,
though it is missing some nicer components people worked on like a better hand
submission component and player sprites sitting around the table.

All in all, I guess a bit of a disappointing finish but I still think the game
is fairly playable and inherently an interesting concept. If I had more energy
or a better ego I think things could have been better.
